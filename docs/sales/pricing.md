## 1. The pricing model and why

**What we charge for: the venue, sized by how many stands it has.** One meter, and nothing else.

- **What counts as a stand:** anything with its own QR poster where food is stored, prepared or served. That means a stand, kitchen, cart, bar or pantry. For a restaurant group, one restaurant counts as one stand. For a festival, one vendor counts as one stand.
- **Closed stands can be paused.** They don't count while paused.
- **Stand teams and crews are always free.** Hundreds of short-term workers use SDX through a QR poster or a link. Charging per person would kill the adoption that makes the product work.
- **Why not per user:** per-seat tools punish frontline use.
  - SafetyCulture's free plan stops at 10 users.
  - Its free guest seats get only 3 inspections for life.
  - Its cheaper Lite seats are capped at 12 inspections a year.
- **Why not per site at restaurant rates:** FoodDocs Lite is $79 per site per month on annual billing. For a 100-stand stadium that comes to $7,900 a month. No stadium will pay that.
- **Why stand bands work:**
  - They follow the real work: posters, setup, training and data all grow with stands.
  - They are easy to count, because the license list is the stand list.
  - The cost per stand drops as venues get bigger, which matches what big buyers expect.
- **What we sell against:** the three-tool stack. Food-safety apps charge per site, inspection apps charge per person, and work-order apps charge per technician. SDX does all three jobs for one venue price.

## 3. Setup, pilot, launch pricing, discounts, payment

### Setup / onboarding fee — always charged (Joxel)
It pays for Joxel's time and screens out buyers who are not serious.
- **Standard setup = Zoom + PDF:** stand list imported from their Excel (the app's 📥 Import), equipment registry, QR posters (print-ready PDF), crew invite links, a Zoom training for inspectors, stand supervisors and crews, and the PDF setup guide.
- **Fees:** Starter $500 · Venue $1,500 · Stadium $3,000 · Portfolio $2,500 per venue (quoted) · Event Pass: included.
- **Founding customers (first 10):** 50% off setup. Nobody gets it free.
- **On-site visit (optional, anywhere in the US):** $750 per day plus travel at cost (flights, hotel, car), booked at least 2 weeks ahead.
- **Printing:** the print-ready PDF is always included; if we print and ship posters, at cost.
- **Custom domain (optional):** the app on their own address, e.g. `inspect.theirvenue.com`. $500 one-time setup + $60 a year (domain, DNS, SSL). They can also point a domain they already own.

### Pilot → paid (the free 30-day pilot stays, with rules)

**Before day 1, get a one-page pilot letter signed. It must name:**
- The stands and people in scope.
- The decision-maker, who must be on the day-30 call.
- 3–4 success metrics, all measured inside SDX. Suggested defaults (agree the numbers with them):
  - Share of stands logging temps on event days
  - Every stand inspected with the full guide
  - Share of problems closed with an AFTER photo within 48 hours
  - Licenses on file for every stand
- The plan, price and start date if the pilot succeeds.

**Pilot length:**
- **For venues:** 30 days or 3 event days, whichever comes later, up to 60 days. An NFL month can hold only 2 home games.
- **For restaurant groups:** 30 days flat.

**Timeline:**
- **Day 15:** check-in using their own numbers.
- **Day 30:** the decision meeting, booked on day 1. Bring a pilot report pulled from the app: inspections, temps logged, stands not scanning, problems closed with photos.

**What happens on day 30:**
- **Yes** → the plan in the letter starts on day 31. Invoice net 30, setup fee included in the first invoice.
- **Not enough event days yet** → one extension, up to 30 days. Only one.
- **No** → the account turns read-only for 30 days. They can export everything to Excel, then it closes. Posters show "pilot ended."
- **Silence** → the letter says the monthly plan starts on day 31 unless they cancel in writing. They can switch to annual within 60 days.

**After the first 10 customers:** switch to a paid pilot. It is 60 days for one month of the plan's monthly price, and 100% of it is credited to year one.

### Payment terms
- **Annual:**
  - A signed order form is required.
  - Invoiced on day 31, or at signing.
  - Net 30, by ACH, check or card.
- **Installments:** Stadium and Portfolio may pay in 2 or 4 installments in advance at the same annual price.
- **Monthly and season:** card on file, billed in advance. Monthly plans cancel with 30 days' notice.
- **Renewal:** auto-renews yearly, with 60 days' notice to cancel. Increases are capped at 5% a year (founding customers are locked through year 3).
- **Growing past the band mid-year:** no surprise bills. If they go more than 10% over, true up at renewal.
- **Late payment:** a reminder at 15 days. Service pauses at 45 days past due. Data is kept.

## 5. Floor, walk-away rules, "too expensive", and when Joxel joins

### Floor prices
- **Plans:** never more than 40% off list. The founding price is the floor:
  - Stadium: $9,540
  - Venue: $5,340
  - Starter: $2,340
- **Portfolio:** never below $8 per stand per month.
- **Event Pass:** never below $590.
- **Season plans:** sold at full monthly price only, never discounted.
- **Minimum deal:** $1,500 total.

### Walk-away rules
1. **Below the floor:** cut scope, not price. Offer fewer stands, one floor or level, or season billing. Otherwise walk.
2. **No signed pilot letter, no pilot.** The letter must have metrics, a conversion price and a named decision-maker.
3. **No budget owner after 2 calls:** stop investing time.
4. **One pilot extension, maximum.** Never "free for a season."
5. **Custom features are never free.** Quote them separately.
6. **Never agree to:**
   - Per-user pricing
   - Per-scan or per-photo fees
   - Exclusivity
   - Giving away source code or IP
7. **Payment terms beyond net 60:** ask for a card or a deposit, or walk.
8. **A buyer who demands SOC 2 before signing:** offer a security one-pager (Firebase/Google Cloud, what data is held, access roles, no card data). Do not fund an audit for one prospect.

### When they say "too expensive"
1. **"Compared to what?"** Ask what they use today: paper, staff time, the cost of their last failed inspection. Let them name the number.
2. **Break it down:** "$15.90 per stand per NFL game, and less once you count your other events."
3. **Anchor:** "FoodDocs Lite is $79 per stand a month. That's $7,900 a month for 100 stands, and it doesn't include crews or work orders."
4. **Shrink, don't cut:** start with one level (Venue band), season billing, or monthly.
5. **Trade:** a founding rate for a case study and references; a price freeze for a longer term; better terms for paying up front.
6. **Let the data decide:** "Run the pilot. Decide on day 30 with your own numbers."
7. **Never drop the price on the first objection.**

| Deal | Who handles it |
|---|---|
| Starter, Venue or Event Pass at list or with standard discounts, under $10,000 first year | Anyone selling can quote and close |
| Any Stadium plan, any deal $10,000–$25,000, any founding slot, any on-site work, any stadium/arena pilot | Joxel joins the second call and approves the quote |

Always check Joxel's calendar before promising an on-site date. He does every setup himself.

### Before your first quote
- **Ownership:** confirm in writing that DS Marketing owns SDX and can sell it. It was built while Joxel worked as an inspector, and buyers' legal teams will ask.
- **Current venue:** do not name the current venue or its operator, or show their data, without written permission. Demo with the demo data.
- **Promises:** only sell features that exist today. That rules out sensors, SSO and push notifications to closed phones.
