Cross-contamination and allergens

about 2 min read5-question check

In this lesson

  • Explain what cross-contamination is and how germs move from one food, surface or hand to another food.
  • Store food in the cooler in the right order: ready-to-eat on top, raw poultry on the bottom.
  • Keep raw and ready-to-eat food apart with separate boards, utensils and gloves, or by washing, rinsing and sanitizing between tasks.
  • Wash fruits and vegetables before cutting, cooking or serving them.
  • Name the 9 major food allergens and prevent allergen cross-contact.
  • Answer allergen questions honestly and know what to do when someone has an allergic reaction.

The lesson

Picture this. A cook cuts raw chicken, wipes the board with a towel, and slices tomatoes on it for the burgers. The chicken germs just moved onto those tomatoes, and nobody cooks a tomato on a burger.

That is cross-contamination: germs moving from one food, surface, or hand to another food. They ride on knives, boards, towels, gloves, and juices that drip in the cooler. I'm Inspector Dex. Let's stop it.

So store food in the cooler by cooking temperature. Ready-to-eat food goes on top. Below it, seafood and whole cuts of beef or pork. Then ground beef and pork. Raw poultry, ground poultry too, goes on the bottom. It needs the highest cooking temperature, and its juices must never drip on other food.

Use separate cutting boards and utensils for raw meat and ready-to-eat food. If you share one, wash, rinse, and sanitize it between tasks. A towel wipe is not enough. Gloves are for one task. Going from raw meat to buns? Wash your hands and put on new gloves.

Fruits and vegetables can carry dirt and germs too. Wash them well in clean water before you cut, cook, or serve them. Use the prep sink, never the hand sink.

Now, allergens. For some people, even a tiny bit of one food can cause a serious reaction. The nine major food allergens are milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish like shrimp and crab, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans, and sesame. Sesame joined the list in 2023.

Allergen cross-contact is when a little of an allergen gets into food that should not have it. Before an allergy order, clean and sanitize the pan, board, and slicer. Use separate fryer oil: fries from the shrimp oil are not shellfish-free. Wash your hands, put on fresh gloves, and use clean, separate utensils.

When a customer asks, 'Does this have peanuts?', be honest. Check the label or the recipe, or ask your manager. Never guess. If nobody is sure, tell the customer. 'I'm not sure' is better than a wrong 'no.'

Know the signs of an allergic reaction: hives or itching, swelling of the lips, tongue, or throat, coughing or trouble breathing, vomiting, or dizziness. It can start within minutes or hours after eating. Call 911 or your venue's emergency number right away, tell your manager, and stay with the person.

The one thing to remember: keep raw food away from ready-to-eat food, and never guess about allergens. Your local rules or your venue may be stricter, so follow them too. See you in the next lesson.

Remember

  • Cross-contamination = germs moving from one food, surface or hand to another food.
  • Cooler order, top to bottom: ready-to-eat → seafood and whole cuts of beef or pork (145°F) → ground beef or pork (155°F) → raw poultry (165°F) on the bottom.
  • Use separate boards and utensils, or wash, rinse and sanitize between raw meat and ready-to-eat food. A towel wipe is not enough.
  • Gloves are for one task. Wash your hands and put on new gloves when you change tasks.
  • Wash fruits and vegetables in clean water before you cut, cook or serve them, in the prep sink and never in the hand sink.
  • The 9 major food allergens: milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans and sesame (sesame since 2023).
  • Prevent allergen cross-contact: clean and sanitize equipment, use separate fryer oil, wash hands and put on fresh gloves, use separate utensils.
  • Never guess about allergens: check the label or the recipe, or ask your manager.
  • Signs of an allergic reaction (hives, swelling, trouble breathing, vomiting, dizziness) can start within minutes or hours after eating: call 911 or your venue's emergency number, tell your manager, stay with the person.

Check yourself

Pick an answer — you will see right away if it is right, and why. Get 4 of 5 right to complete the lesson.

1. In the cooler, which food goes on the bottom shelf?

Raw poultry needs the highest cooking temperature (165°F), so it goes lowest, where its juices cannot drip on other food.

2. You just cut raw chicken. Now you need to slice tomatoes on the same board. What do you do first?

A shared board must be washed, rinsed and sanitized between raw meat and ready-to-eat food; a wipe or a rinse does not remove the germs.

3. Which food became the ninth major food allergen in 2023?

Sesame was added by the FASTER Act of 2021 and has been a major food allergen since January 1, 2023.

4. The fries are cooked in the same fryer oil as the shrimp. A customer with a shellfish allergy asks if the fries are safe. What do you say?

Shared fryer oil carries shellfish onto the fries, so tell the truth and offer to ask your manager.

5. After eating, a customer's lips swell and they have trouble breathing. What do you do?

Swelling and trouble breathing can be a serious allergic reaction, so call 911 or your venue's emergency number right away.

Where this comes from
  • Raw animal foods must be kept separate from ready-to-eat food (stored below it), and different types of raw animal food must be separated so one cannot contaminate another; storing by cooking temperature (ready-to-eat on top, poultry at the bottom) is how this is commonly done.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-302.11 Packaged and Unpackaged Food – Separation, Packaging, and Segregation
  • Minimum cooking temperatures: fish and intact meat (steaks, chops) 145°F for 15 seconds (whole roasts follow the time and temperature table in 3-401.11(B), e.g. 145°F for 4 minutes); ground (comminuted) meat and fish 155°F for 17 seconds; poultry 165°F for less than 1 second.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-401.11(A)(1)(b), (A)(2), (A)(3) and (B) Raw Animal Foods / whole meat roasts
  • Food-contact surfaces and utensils must be cleaned before use with a different type of raw animal food and each time there is a change from working with raw food to ready-to-eat food, and sanitized after cleaning and before use.FDA Food Code 2022, 4-602.11(A) Equipment Food-Contact Surfaces and Utensils (frequency); 4-702.11 Before Use After Cleaning
  • Single-use gloves are used for only one task (such as raw animal food or ready-to-eat food) and thrown away when damaged, soiled, or when the task is interrupted.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-304.15(A) Gloves, Use Limitation
  • Food employees wash their hands when switching between raw food and ready-to-eat food, and before putting on gloves to start a task that involves food.FDA Food Code 2022, 2-301.14 When to Wash
  • Raw fruits and vegetables must be thoroughly washed in water before being cut, combined with other ingredients, cooked, served, or offered as ready-to-eat food.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-302.15(A) Washing Fruits and Vegetables
  • A handwashing sink may be used only for washing hands.FDA Food Code 2022, 5-205.11(B) Using a Handwashing Sink
  • The major food allergens are milk, egg, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, wheat, peanuts, soybeans and sesame, and foods that contain protein from them.FDA Food Code 2022, 1-201.10(B) definition of 'Major food allergen'
  • Sesame was added as the ninth major food allergen by the FASTER Act of 2021 (signed April 23, 2021), effective January 1, 2023.Food Allergy Safety, Treatment, Education, and Research (FASTER) Act of 2021, Public Law 117-11
  • The person in charge must be able to describe the major food allergens and the symptoms they can cause, and must make sure employees are trained in food allergy awareness for their duties.FDA Food Code 2022, 2-102.11(C) Demonstration of knowledge; 2-103.11 Person in Charge duties
  • The permit holder must notify consumers in writing (for example brochures, menu or deli-case notes, labels, table tents or placards) of the major food allergens that are ingredients in unpackaged food items served or sold to them.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-602.12(C) Other Forms of Information (added in the 2022 edition)
  • Allergen cross-contact is prevented by cleaning and sanitizing food-contact surfaces and keeping allergen foods separate; dedicated fryer oil and separate utensils are best practices for this, not a separately numbered Food Code section.FDA Food Code 2022, 4-602.11 and 3-302.11 (cleaning and separation)
  • Food allergy symptoms can include hives, itching, swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, coughing or wheezing, trouble breathing, vomiting, dizziness or fainting; they usually appear within minutes to a couple of hours after eating, and a severe reaction (anaphylaxis) can be life-threatening and needs emergency help.U.S. FDA, 'Food Allergies: What You Need to Know' (fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/food-allergies-what-you-need-know)
  • Calling the venue's own emergency number or on-site medical team (as well as 911) is a venue procedure, not a Food Code requirement; follow your venue's emergency plan.Course rule: your local rules or your venue may be stricter (venue procedure)
