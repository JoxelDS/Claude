Receiving, storage, labels and dates

about 2 min read5-question check

In this lesson

  • Accept food only from approved sources.
  • Check every delivery: cold TCS food at 41°F or below, frozen food frozen solid, and reject damaged packages, signs of pests and bad smells.
  • Store food at least 6 inches off the floor, rotate stock with FIFO, and leave space in coolers and freezers so cold air can move.
  • Label working containers with the food's common name, date-mark ready-to-eat TCS food that is made here or opened from a package and kept more than 24 hours (7 days max, the day it was made or opened is day 1), and throw it out when the date passes.

The lesson

One hour before gates open, a delivery arrives. The driver says: "Just sign here." Wait! Before you sign, you check. I'm Inspector Dex. Today: receiving, storage, labels and dates.

First, all food must come from an approved source: a supplier that is inspected and follows the food laws. No food made at home. No "good deal" from a car trunk.

Next, use your thermometer. Cold TCS food, like raw chicken, deli meat or cut melon, must arrive at 41°F or below. Some foods have their own rule: shell eggs can come in a truck with the air at 45°F or below. Cold food at 50°F? Say no.

Frozen food must arrive frozen solid. Large ice crystals, frozen liquid at the bottom of the box, or water stains are signs that it thawed and froze again. Reject it.

Then check the package and the food. Reject swollen, leaking or badly dented cans, torn bags and broken seals. Reject any sign of pests, like droppings or chewed boxes. Off smell or slime? Reject. Tell your supervisor.

When it passes, put cold and frozen food away first. Keep food boxes off the floor: store food at least 6 inches off the floor, on a shelf or rack. Older food goes to the front, new food to the back. That's FIFO: first in, first out.

Labels. When you move food out of its original package into a working container, like flour, sugar, salt or oil, write the common name of the food on it. Sugar and salt look the same. A label stops a big mistake.

Dates. Ready-to-eat TCS food kept more than 24 hours needs a date. That means food you make here, like cooked rice or cut melon, and packages you open, like deli meat. At 41°F or below, you have 7 days at most. The day you make it or open it is day 1. Made on Monday? The last day is Sunday.

Date passed? Throw it out. Not tomorrow. Not "it still smells fine." Food that needs a date but has none? Out too. Your local rules or your venue may be stricter. Follow the stricter rule.

Last, don't overload the cooler or freezer. Cold air must move around the food. Boxes packed against the walls and the fan block the air, and food can warm up. Leave space, and log the cooler temperature.

The one thing to remember: never just sign. Check every delivery, and if it doesn't pass, it doesn't come in. See you next lesson.

Remember

  • All food must come from an approved source. No food made at home. (FDA Food Code 3-201.11)
  • Cold TCS food must arrive at 41°F or below. Some foods have their own rule: shell eggs may arrive in a truck with the air at 45°F or below. (3-202.11)
  • Frozen food must arrive frozen solid. Large ice crystals, frozen liquid in the box or water stains mean it thawed: reject it. (3-202.11)
  • Reject swollen, leaking or badly dented cans, torn bags, broken seals, signs of pests, off smells and slime, and tell your supervisor. (3-202.15, 3-101.11)
  • Put cold and frozen food away first, and store all food at least 6 inches (15 cm) off the floor. (3-305.11)
  • Exceptions: kegs, cases of canned or bottled drinks and milk in plastic crates may sit on a clean, dry floor, and dollies and pallets are allowed. Your local rules or your venue may be stricter. (3-305.11, 4-204.122)
  • FIFO: older food in front, new food in back, use the oldest first.
  • Label working containers (flour, sugar, salt, oil…) with the common name of the food. (3-302.12)
  • Ready-to-eat TCS food kept more than 24 hours gets a date, both food made here and packages you open: 7 days max at 41°F or below, the day it was made or opened is day 1, and never past the maker's use-by date. (3-501.17)
  • Throw food out when its date passes, or when it needs a date and has none. (3-501.18)
  • Don't overload coolers and freezers: cold air must move around the food.
  • Your local rules or your venue may be stricter. Follow the stricter rule.

Check yourself

Pick an answer — you will see right away if it is right, and why. Get 4 of 5 right to complete the lesson.

1. A delivery of raw chicken arrives. What is the highest temperature you can accept?

Cold TCS food like raw chicken must be received at 41°F or below (FDA Food Code 3-202.11).

2. You open a box of frozen fries and see large ice crystals and frozen liquid at the bottom. What do you do?

Frozen food must arrive frozen solid; large ice crystals and frozen liquid show it thawed and was frozen again before it reached you (FDA Food Code 3-202.11).

3. How high off the floor must food be stored?

Food must be stored at least 6 inches (15 cm) above the floor (FDA Food Code 3-305.11).

4. You make cooked rice on Tuesday and keep it at 41°F or below. What is the last day you can use it?

The day it is made counts as day 1, so Tuesday is day 1 and Monday is day 7, the last day (FDA Food Code 3-501.17).

5. You pour sugar from its bag into a bin. What must the bin have?

Working containers must show the common name of the food (FDA Food Code 3-302.12), because sugar and salt look the same.

  2. 2. Reject it, because these are signs it thawed and froze again — Frozen food must arrive frozen solid; large ice crystals and frozen liquid show it thawed and was frozen again before it reached you (FDA Food Code 3-202.11).

Where this comes from
  • Food must be obtained from sources that comply with law, and food prepared in a private home may not be used or offered in a food establishment.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-201.11 (A) and (B)
  • Refrigerated TCS food must be at 5°C (41°F) or below when received, unless a different temperature is set by the law that governs its distribution.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-202.11 (A) and (B)
  • Raw (shell) eggs must be received in refrigerated equipment that keeps an ambient air temperature of 7°C (45°F) or less.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-202.11 (C)
  • Food labeled frozen and shipped frozen must be received frozen, and TCS food must be free of evidence of previous temperature abuse when received.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-202.11 (E) and (F)
  • Large ice crystals, frozen liquid in the package and water stains are signs of thawing and refreezing (temperature abuse).Common receiving guidance applying FDA Food Code 2022, 3-202.11 (F); the Food Code does not list these signs by name
  • Food packages must be in good condition and protect the contents from adulteration or contaminants.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-202.15 (Package integrity)
  • Food must be safe and unadulterated (basis for rejecting food with signs of pests, off odors or slime).FDA Food Code 2022, 3-101.11
  • Food must be stored at least 15 cm (6 inches) above the floor.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-305.11 (A)(3)
  • Pressurized beverage containers, cased food in waterproof containers such as bottles or cans, and milk containers in plastic crates may be stored on a floor that is clean and not exposed to floor moisture; food in packages and working containers may be stored less than 6 inches above the floor on case lot handling equipment such as dollies, pallets, racks and skids.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-305.11 (B) and (C); 4-204.122
  • Working containers holding food removed from its original package (such as flour, sugar, salt, cooking oil, spices) must be identified with the common name of the food, unless the food is readily and unmistakably recognized (such as dry pasta).FDA Food Code 2022, 3-302.12
  • Refrigerated ready-to-eat TCS food prepared and held more than 24 hours must be date-marked for use within a maximum of 7 days at 5°C (41°F) or less; the day of preparation counts as Day 1.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-501.17 (A)
  • Refrigerated ready-to-eat TCS food prepared and packaged by a food processing plant, once its original container is opened and held more than 24 hours, must be date-marked for a maximum of 7 days at 5°C (41°F) or less; the day the container is opened counts as Day 1, and the date may not exceed a manufacturer's use-by date based on food safety.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-501.17 (B)
  • Ready-to-eat TCS food must be discarded when it exceeds the 7-day limit, or when it is in a container that does not bear the required date or day.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-501.18 (A)
  • TCS food in cold holding must be kept at 5°C (41°F) or less.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-501.16 (A)(2)
  • Cold-holding equipment must be sufficient in number and capacity to keep food at the required temperatures (basis for not overloading coolers so cold air can move).FDA Food Code 2022, 4-301.11
  • FIFO (first in, first out) is a standard stock-rotation practice; it is not a named Food Code section but supports date marking and discard.Industry practice; supports FDA Food Code 2022, 3-501.17 and 3-501.18
  • Raw chicken, deli meat, cut melons and cooked rice are TCS foods.FDA Food Code 2022, 1-201.10 (B), definition of 'Time/temperature control for safety food'
