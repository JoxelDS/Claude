Cooking, cooling and reheating

about 3 min read5-question check

In this lesson

  • Name the minimum internal cooking temperatures for poultry, stuffed pasta and foods stuffed with meat, fish or poultry; ground beef, pork and fish; steaks, pork and fish; eggs; whole roasts; and plant foods for hot holding.
  • Check food temperature with a thermometer in the thickest part of the food.
  • Cool cooked food in two stages: 135°F to 70°F within 2 hours, then to 41°F or below within 6 hours total.
  • Pick a fast cooling method and never cool a big pot on the counter.
  • Reheat food for hot holding to 165°F for 15 seconds within 2 hours.
  • Thaw frozen food using one of the four safe methods.

The lesson

Hi, I'm Inspector Dex. Halftime is over, and there is a big pot of chili left. Someone leaves it on the counter to cool. It looks fine, but germs can grow in it fast. Today you'll learn to cook, cool and reheat food safely.

First, cooking. Color does not tell you food is safe. Only a thermometer can. Put the probe in the thickest part. Germs grow best between 41 and 135 degrees. The right cooking temperature kills most of them.

Chicken, turkey, ground chicken or turkey, stuffed pasta, and anything stuffed with meat, fish or poultry, like peppers stuffed with ground beef: 165 degrees. The moment it reaches 165, it is safe.

Burgers and other ground beef or pork, ground fish, injected or tenderized meat, and eggs cooked ahead to hold hot: 155 degrees for 17 seconds.

Steaks, pork chops, fish fillets, and eggs cracked for a customer to eat right away: 145 degrees for 15 seconds. A whole roast: 145 degrees, held for 4 minutes.

Vegetables, fruit, rice and beans that go into hot holding: 135 degrees. Your local rules or your venue may be stricter.

Now, cooling. Write down the start time. Hot food must drop from 135 to 70 degrees within 2 hours. Then it must reach 41 degrees or colder, within 6 hours total from the start. Not at 70 after 2 hours? Reheat it to 165 and start cooling again, or throw it out. Not at 41 by 6 hours? Throw it out. Tell your supervisor.

How do you cool food fast? Split it into shallow pans. Or set the pot in an ice bath and stir it. Use an ice wand, or a blast chiller if you have one. Never leave a big pot on the counter to cool. A big pot stays warm in the middle for a long time.

Reheating. When cooked and cooled food goes back into hot holding, reheat it to 165 degrees for 15 seconds. Get it there fast, within 2 hours. Then keep it at 135 degrees or hotter.

Last, thawing. There are four safe ways. In the cooler, at 41 degrees or colder. Under cold running water, 70 degrees or colder, with the food fully under water. In the microwave, only if you cook it right away. Or as part of cooking, like frozen burger patties on the grill. Never thaw food on the counter.

Write every reading in your temperature log, so your team and your inspector can see the food was safe.

The one thing to remember: never guess. Cook it hot, cool it fast, reheat it to 165, and check every step with a thermometer.

Remember

  • Color does not show food is safe: check with a thermometer in the thickest part of the food.
  • 165°F (less than 1 second): chicken, turkey and other poultry (ground poultry too), stuffed pasta, and any food stuffed with meat, fish or poultry.
  • 155°F for 17 seconds: burgers and other ground beef or pork, ground fish, injected or tenderized meat, and eggs cooked ahead for hot holding.
  • 145°F for 15 seconds: steaks, pork chops, fish, and eggs cracked for a customer to eat right away. Whole roasts: 145°F for 4 minutes. Fruit, vegetables, rice and beans for hot holding: 135°F.
  • Cooling: from 135°F to 70°F within 2 hours, then to 41°F or below within 6 hours total. Not at 70°F after 2 hours? Reheat to 165°F and start cooling again, or throw it out. Not at 41°F within 6 hours? Throw it out — and tell your supervisor.
  • Cool fast with shallow pans, an ice bath and stirring, an ice wand, or a blast chiller. Never cool a big pot on the counter.
  • Reheat cooked and cooled food for hot holding to 165°F for 15 seconds, within 2 hours, then hold it at 135°F or hotter.
  • Thaw safely: in the cooler at 41°F or below, under cold running water at 70°F or below, in the microwave only if cooked right away, or as part of cooking. Never on the counter.
  • Your local rules or your venue may be stricter.

Check yourself

Pick an answer — you will see right away if it is right, and why. Get 4 of 5 right to complete the lesson.

1. Which of these foods must be cooked to at least 165°F?

Poultry like chicken wings must reach 165°F; steak and fish need 145°F for 15 seconds, and rice for hot holding needs 135°F (FDA Food Code 3-401.11 and 3-401.13).

2. You are cooling a pan of cooked chili. What is the most time you have to get it from 135°F down to 70°F?

Cooked food must reach 70°F within 2 hours, and then 41°F or below within 6 hours total (FDA Food Code 3-501.14).

3. Yesterday's cooked and cooled chili is going back into the steam table. How must you reheat it?

Food that was cooked and cooled must be reheated to 165°F for 15 seconds within 2 hours before hot holding (FDA Food Code 3-403.11).

4. Which is a safe way to thaw frozen chicken?

Thawing in the cooler at 41°F or below is one of the four safe ways; the others are cold running water at 70°F or below, the microwave if cooked right away, or as part of cooking (FDA Food Code 3-501.13).

5. What is the best way to cool a big pot of soup?

Shallow pans and ice baths pull the heat out fast, while a big pot stays warm in the middle too long (FDA Food Code 3-501.15).

Where this comes from
  • Poultry, stuffed fish, meat, pasta or poultry, and stuffing containing fish, meat or poultry must be cooked to 165°F for less than 1 second (instantaneous).FDA Food Code 2022, §3-401.11(A)(3)
  • Comminuted (ground) meat and fish — such as ground beef or pork — injected or mechanically tenderized meat, and raw eggs not prepared for immediate service (for example, eggs cooked ahead for hot holding) must be cooked to 155°F for 17 seconds. Ground poultry is poultry and must reach 165°F.FDA Food Code 2022, §3-401.11(A)(2)
  • Whole-muscle meat (beef, pork), fish, and raw shell eggs broken and prepared for immediate service must be cooked to 145°F for 15 seconds.FDA Food Code 2022, §3-401.11(A)(1)
  • Whole meat roasts may be cooked to 145°F and held for 4 minutes (from the time-temperature table for roasts).FDA Food Code 2022, §3-401.11(B)
  • Fruits and vegetables (plant foods) cooked for hot holding must be cooked to 135°F.FDA Food Code 2022, §3-401.13
  • Time/temperature control for safety food must be held at 135°F or above (hot) or 41°F or below (cold).FDA Food Code 2022, §3-501.16(A)
  • Cooked TCS food must be cooled from 135°F to 70°F within 2 hours, and from 135°F to 41°F or less within a total of 6 hours.FDA Food Code 2022, §3-501.14(A)
  • When cooling misses a limit — not at 70°F within the first 2 hours, or not at 41°F within 6 hours total — the usual corrective action is: within the first 2 hours, reheat to 165°F and start cooling again, or discard; after the 6-hour limit, discard.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-501.14(A) (the limits); corrective actions as in the FDA Food Code Annex 4 HACCP guidance — local codes may set their own
  • Approved cooling methods include shallow pans, separating food into smaller portions, rapid cooling equipment, stirring in a container placed in an ice water bath, containers that help heat transfer, and adding ice as an ingredient; containers in cooling equipment are loosely covered or uncovered and arranged for heat transfer.FDA Food Code 2022, §3-501.15
  • TCS food that is cooked, cooled and reheated for hot holding must be reheated so all parts reach at least 165°F for 15 seconds, and the reheating must be done rapidly so the food is between 41°F and 165°F for no more than 2 hours.FDA Food Code 2022, §3-403.11(A) and (D)
  • TCS food may be thawed under refrigeration at 41°F or less; completely submerged under running water at 70°F or below; in a microwave when immediately followed by conventional cooking with no interruption; or as part of the cooking process.FDA Food Code 2022, §3-501.13
  • A food temperature measuring device must be provided and readily accessible for measuring the temperature of food.FDA Food Code 2022, §4-302.12(A)
