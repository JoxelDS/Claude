Pests, chemicals and your inspection

about 2 min read5-question check

In this lesson

  • Spot the signs of pests and keep pests out of the stand.
  • Report pests to a manager and leave pesticides to the licensed pest control operator.
  • Store, label and use chemicals safely.
  • Know what an inspector checks, and build daily habits that make every inspection easy.

The lesson

It's closing time. You move a box of buns, and behind it you see tiny black droppings. What do you do? Today: pests, chemicals, and what an inspector looks at, so every visit is easy.

First, learn the signs of pests. Droppings. Gnaw marks on bags and boxes. A bad, musty smell. Nests made of shredded paper or cloth. Live or dead insects in warm, dark, wet places, like under the sink or behind the cooler.

Pests want three things: food, water, and a way in. Take all three away. Keep doors closed. Report gaps and holes. Take out the trash and keep the lids on. Keep floors clean and dry. Store food covered, and at least six inches off the floor.

See a sign of pests? Take a photo and tell your manager right away. Food that pests got into goes in the trash. And never spray bug killer yourself. Pesticides are a job for the licensed pest control operator. Your local rules or your venue may be stricter.

Now, chemicals: cleaner, sanitizer, degreaser. Store them below and away from food, ice, and anything that touches food. Never on a shelf above food. One drip can make someone sick.

Every spray bottle and bucket needs a label with the chemical's name. No label? Don't use it. Ask your manager. Use each chemical only the way its label says. And know where the Safety Data Sheets are kept. They say what to do if a chemical splashes your eyes or skin.

What do I check when I visit? The hand sink: soap, paper towels or a hand dryer, and used only for washing hands. Cold food at 41°F or below. Hot food at 135°F or above. A thermometer that works. Date labels on cold ready-to-eat food you keep more than a day. Your local rules or your venue may be stricter.

Sanitizer buckets at the right strength. I test them with a strip. Raw meat stored below ready-to-eat food. Clean surfaces. No signs of pests. And healthy staff, with clean hands, hair under a hat or hair net, and no eating at the prep table.

Here is the secret: an inspection is just a normal day, checked. Check temperatures, keep sanitizer fresh, label and date, clean as you go. With SDX Inspect, you can scan your stand poster to log temperatures or report a problem, like a pest.

The one thing to remember: close it, clean it, label it, report it. Every shift. Then every inspection is easy.

Remember

  • Signs of pests: droppings, gnaw marks, a bad musty smell, nests, live or dead insects.
  • Keep pests out: close doors and report gaps and holes (6-202.15), take out the trash and keep lids on (5-501.113, 5-502.11), keep areas clean and dry with no hiding places (6-501.111), store food covered (3-302.11) and at least 6 inches (15 cm) off the floor (3-305.11).
  • See a pest sign? Take a photo and tell your manager right away. Throw away food that pests got into (3-701.11). Leave pesticides to the licensed pest control operator. The Food Code allows pesticides only as the label and the law say, and restricted-use pesticides only by a certified applicator (7-202.12). Your local rules or your venue may be stricter.
  • Store chemicals below and away from food, ice and food-contact surfaces, never above them (7-201.11).
  • Label every spray bottle and bucket with the chemical's name (7-102.11). No label = don't use it. Use chemicals only as the label says, and know where the Safety Data Sheets are.
  • On a visit the inspector checks: hand sinks with soap and paper towels or a hand dryer, used only for hands (5-205.11, 6-301.12); cold food 41°F or below and hot food 135°F or above (3-501.16); thermometers; date labels on cold ready-to-eat food kept more than a day (3-501.17); sanitizer strength; raw meat below ready-to-eat food; clean surfaces; pests; staff health and hygiene.
  • Good daily habits make every inspection easy. Your local rules or your venue may be stricter.

Check yourself

Pick an answer — you will see right away if it is right, and why. Get 4 of 5 right to complete the lesson.

1. How high off the floor should food be stored?

Food is stored at least 6 inches (15 cm) off the floor so it stays protected and the floor can be cleaned (FDA Food Code 3-305.11).

2. Behind a box you find droppings and gnaw marks. What do you do?

Pest signs go to the manager right away. Pesticides are a job for the licensed pest control operator: the Food Code allows them only as the label and the law say (7-202.12), and most venues allow no one else.

3. Where do cleaning chemicals go?

Chemicals must be kept apart from food and never stored above food or food-contact surfaces, so a drip or spill cannot reach them (7-201.11).

4. You find a spray bottle with no label. What do you do?

Every working container must be labeled with the chemical's name (7-102.11); if you don't know what it is, don't use it.

5. On a visit, the inspector checks holding temperatures. Which is correct?

The Food Code holds cold food at 41°F (5°C) or below and hot food at 135°F (57°C) or above (3-501.16).

Where this comes from
  • The premises must be kept free of insects, rodents and other pests, and the operation must routinely inspect incoming deliveries and the premises for evidence of pests and eliminate harborage conditions.FDA Food Code 2022, 6-501.111 Controlling Pests
  • Outer openings (doors, windows, gaps) must be protected against the entry of insects and rodents, e.g. doors kept closed and openings sealed.FDA Food Code 2022, 6-202.15 Outer Openings, Protected
  • Inside, refuse receptacles that hold food residue are kept covered when not in continuous use and after they are filled; outside receptacles have tight-fitting lids or doors (5-501.113). Refuse is removed often enough to prevent odors and pest attraction (5-502.11).FDA Food Code 2022, 5-501.113 Covering Receptacles; 5-502.11 Frequency of Removal
  • Food must be stored in a clean, dry location where it is not exposed to splash, dust or other contamination, at least 15 cm (6 inches) above the floor.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-305.11 Food Storage
  • Food that is unsafe, adulterated or contaminated (such as food pests have gotten into) must be discarded.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-701.11 Discarding or Reconditioning Unsafe, Adulterated, or Contaminated Food
  • Poisonous or toxic materials, including pesticides, must be used according to law and the manufacturer's label; a restricted-use pesticide may be applied only by a certified applicator or a person under their direct supervision. Many venues allow only the licensed pest control operator to apply any pesticide — your local rules or your venue may be stricter.FDA Food Code 2022, 7-202.12 Conditions of Use
  • Poisonous or toxic materials must be separated from food by spacing or partitioning and must not be stored above food, equipment, utensils, linens or single-service and single-use articles.FDA Food Code 2022, 7-201.11 Separation
  • Working containers of cleaners and sanitizers taken from bulk supplies must be clearly and individually labeled with the common name of the material.FDA Food Code 2022, 7-102.11 Common Name
  • Employers must keep Safety Data Sheets for hazardous chemicals readily accessible to employees during each work shift.OSHA Hazard Communication Standard, 29 CFR 1910.1200(g)(8)
  • A handwashing sink must be accessible at all times and used for no purpose other than handwashing, and it must be supplied with hand cleanser and a hand-drying provision: individual disposable towels, a continuous towel system, a heated-air hand dryer or an air-knife hand dryer.FDA Food Code 2022, 5-205.11 Using a Handwashing Sink; 6-301.11 Handwashing Cleanser; 6-301.12 Hand Drying Provision
  • Time/temperature control for safety (TCS) food must be held cold at 5°C (41°F) or less, or hot at 57°C (135°F) or above.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-501.16 Time/Temperature Control for Safety Food, Hot and Cold Holding
  • Food temperature measuring devices must be provided and readily accessible, and cold and hot holding units must have a temperature measuring device.FDA Food Code 2022, 4-302.12 Food Temperature Measuring Devices; 4-204.112 Temperature Measuring Devices
  • Refrigerated ready-to-eat TCS food prepared on site, or opened from a commercial package, and held more than 24 hours must be date marked (no more than 7 days at 41°F or below, counting the day of preparation or opening as day 1).FDA Food Code 2022, 3-501.17 Ready-to-Eat, TCS Food, Date Marking
  • Chemical sanitizer must be used at the concentration the label and the Code specify, a test kit must be provided to measure sanitizer concentration, and wiping cloths are held in sanitizer at that concentration.FDA Food Code 2022, 4-501.114 Chemical Sanitization; 4-302.14 Sanitizing Solutions, Testing Devices; 3-304.14 Wiping Cloths
  • Raw animal foods must be separated from ready-to-eat foods during storage, for example by storing raw meat below ready-to-eat food, and food is protected from cross-contamination by storing it in packages, covered containers or wrappings.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-302.11 Packaged and Unpackaged Food — Separation, Packaging, and Segregation
  • Food-contact surfaces must be cleaned at the frequency the Code requires (at least every 4 hours when in constant use with TCS food).FDA Food Code 2022, 4-602.11 Equipment Food-Contact Surfaces and Utensils
  • Food employees must keep hands clean, wear hair restraints (hats, hair coverings or nets), eat and drink only in designated areas away from exposed food, and report certain illnesses and symptoms to the person in charge.FDA Food Code 2022, 2-301.11 Clean Condition; 2-402.11 Effectiveness (Hair Restraints); 2-401.11 Eating, Drinking, or Using Tobacco; 2-201.11 Responsibility of Permit Holder, Person in Charge, and Conditional Employees
