Time and temperature control

about 2 min read5-question check

In this lesson

  • Name the temperature danger zone: 41°F–135°F (5°C–57°C).
  • Hold food that needs temperature control cold at 41°F (5°C) or below, or hot at 135°F (57°C) or above.
  • Take a food temperature the right way, and clean and sanitize the probe.
  • Check a thermometer with the ice-point method (32°F / 0°C).
  • Use the 4-hour time rule correctly and take a corrective action when a reading is out of range.

The lesson

Game day. Long line. Nobody has checked these hot dogs since the gates opened. I check one: 118 degrees. Would you serve it?

Germs that make people sick grow fast between 41 and 135 degrees Fahrenheit. We call it the danger zone. Keep food out of it.

Cold food stays at 41 or below. Hot food stays at 135 or above. That's the Food Code rule for food that needs temperature control, like meat, cooked rice and cut melon. Your local rules or your venue may be stricter. Follow the stricter one.

Use a dial thermometer with a metal stem, or a digital one. Push the probe into the thickest part of the food. On a dial one, go in at least to the dimple. Wait until the reading stops moving.

Before and after every use, clean the probe, sanitize it, and let it air-dry. A dirty probe can carry germs from raw chicken into ready-to-eat food.

Thermometers can drift, so check yours with ice. Fill a cup with crushed ice and cold water, and stir. Put the stem in the middle, not touching the cup. It should read 32 degrees. If not, adjust it as the maker says, or use another. Many venues check every shift and after a drop.

How often? The Food Code says holding temperatures must be checked routinely. A common rule is at least every 4 hours. Many venues check every 2 hours, so there's time to fix a problem. Follow your venue's rule.

Some food sits out on purpose, like grab-and-go sandwiches. That's only allowed with a written plan. It must start at 41 or below, or 135 or above. Mark the throw-away time. Serve it within 4 hours, or throw it away. Putting it back in the cooler does not reset the clock.

Back to our hot dogs. 118 is out of range. That needs a corrective action. Stop serving. How long has it been? Your venue's plan sets the limit. Inside that limit, reheat hot food fast to 165 degrees for 15 seconds, or chill cold food fast to 41 or below. Don't know? Throw it out. These? Nobody checked, so they go. Then get the warmer fixed, tell your supervisor, and log what you did.

The one thing to remember: cold at 41 or below, hot at 135 or above. Don't guess. Measure it, write it down, and fix it when it's wrong.

Remember

  • Danger zone: 41°F–135°F (5°C–57°C). Germs that make people sick grow fast there.
  • Food that needs temperature control (meat, dairy, cooked rice, cut melons, cut tomatoes, cut leafy greens): cold at 41°F (5°C) or below, hot at 135°F (57°C) or above (Food Code 3-501.16). Your local rules or your venue may be stricter.
  • Probe the thickest part of the food. On a dial thermometer, go in at least to the dimple. Wait until the reading stops moving.
  • Clean and sanitize the probe before and after every use, and let it air-dry.
  • Ice-point check: crushed ice + cold water = 32°F (0°C). If the reading is off, adjust it as the maker says or use another thermometer (Food Code 4-502.11).
  • Check holding temperatures at least every 4 hours — a common rule; many venues check every 2 hours. Follow your venue's rule.
  • Time as a control, 4-hour option: only with a written plan. Food starts at 41°F or below or 135°F or above, is marked with its throw-away time, and is served or thrown away within 4 hours (Food Code 3-501.19(A)–(B)). Your local rules or your venue may be stricter.
  • Reading out of range? Stop serving and find out how long. If your plan allows it, reheat hot food fast, to 165°F for 15 seconds within 2 hours, or chill cold food to 41°F or below. If you don't know how long, throw it out. Get the cause fixed, tell your supervisor, write it down.

Check yourself

Pick an answer — you will see right away if it is right, and why. Get 4 of 5 right to complete the lesson.

1. What is the temperature danger zone?

Germs grow fast between 41°F and 135°F, so cold food stays at 41°F or below and hot food at 135°F or above (Food Code 3-501.16).

2. Where do you put the probe to check a food's temperature?

The thickest part is the slowest to heat up or cool down, so it shows the real temperature of the food.

3. In the ice-point check, what should a correct thermometer read in the ice water?

Crushed ice and water sit at 32°F (0°C); if the reading is off, adjust it as the maker says or use another thermometer (Food Code 4-502.11).

4. Sandwiches leave the cooler at 11:00 AM under a written 4-hour time plan. When must they be served or thrown away?

Under the 4-hour option, food must be served or thrown away within 4 hours of leaving temperature control (Food Code 3-501.19(B)).

5. The hot dogs in the warmer read 118°F and nobody knows how long they have been like that. What do you do?

118°F is below 135°F, and when you don't know how long food has been out of range, the safe choice is to throw it out and fix the cause.

Where this comes from
  • TCS (time/temperature control for safety) food includes raw or heat-treated animal foods (e.g., meat, most dairy), heat-treated plant foods (e.g., cooked rice), raw seed sprouts, cut melons, cut leafy greens, cut tomatoes or mixtures of cut tomatoes, and garlic-in-oil mixtures. The holding rules apply to TCS food.FDA Food Code 2022, 1-201.10(B) definition of Time/Temperature Control for Safety Food
  • TCS food must be held hot at 135°F (57°C) or above, or cold at 41°F (5°C) or below.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-501.16(A)(1)–(2)
  • The 'danger zone' taught here is the range between those two holding limits, 41°F–135°F (5°C–57°C). 'Danger zone' is a training term; the Food Code itself states the holding limits.Derived from FDA Food Code 2022, 3-501.16(A)
  • Food temperature measuring devices must be provided and readily accessible for checking food temperatures.FDA Food Code 2022, 4-302.12(A)
  • A food temperature measuring device must be cleaned before it is used or stored; utensils and food-contact surfaces are sanitized before use after cleaning, then air-dried (not cloth-dried).FDA Food Code 2022, 4-602.11(A), 4-702.11, 4-901.11
  • On a bimetallic stemmed thermometer the sensing area runs from the tip to the dimple, so the stem goes in at least to the dimple; the reading is taken in the thickest part of the food after it stops moving.Common thermometer practice and manufacturer instructions (not a Food Code section)
  • Food temperature measuring devices must be calibrated according to the manufacturer's specifications as necessary to keep them accurate.FDA Food Code 2022, 4-502.11(B)
  • Ice-point method: a well-stirred mix of crushed ice and water is 32°F (0°C) at normal pressure; the stem goes in at least to the dimple, not touching the cup, and is read once the needle stops (about 30 seconds). Calibrating every shift and after a drop is a common practice / venue rule, not a Food Code requirement.Freezing point of water; standard ice-point calibration practice (method not specified in the Food Code)
  • The person in charge must make sure employees maintain hot- and cold-holding temperatures through daily oversight of routine temperature monitoring. The Food Code sets no fixed check interval; every 4 hours (or every 2 hours) is a common practice / venue rule.FDA Food Code 2022, 2-103.11 (duties of the person in charge)
  • Time as a public health control, 4-hour option: written procedures prepared in advance and available to the regulatory authority; food starts at 41°F or less or 135°F or more when removed from temperature control; marked with the time 4 hours past removal; served or discarded within 4 hours; unmarked or over-time food is discarded. A separate 6-hour option for cold food only (starts at 41°F or less, never above 70°F, monitored and marked) exists and is not taught in this lesson.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-501.19(A)–(B); 6-hour option 3-501.19(C)
  • State and local adoptions of the Food Code vary; some add approval steps or limit which foods may use time as a control, so the lesson tells staff their local rules or venue may be stricter.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-501.19(A) (written procedures); state and local adoptions vary
  • Reheating for hot holding: all parts of the food reach at least 165°F (74°C) for 15 seconds, and reheating must be done rapidly — no more than 2 hours between 41°F and 165°F. In this lesson it is used as a corrective action only where the venue's written plan allows it.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-403.11(A) and (D)
  • Corrective actions for out-of-range readings are set in the operation's food safety plan (active managerial control, HACCP principles).FDA Food Code 2022, Annex 4
