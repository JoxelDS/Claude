Cleaning and sanitizing

about 2 min read5-question check

In this lesson

  • Explain the difference between cleaning and sanitizing, and why you always clean first.
  • Know when surfaces that touch food must be cleaned and sanitized, including the 4-hour rule.
  • Wash items at a 3-compartment sink in the right order: scrape, wash (110°F or hotter), rinse, sanitize, air dry.
  • Check sanitizer strength with a test strip, keep wiping cloths in sanitizer, and follow the dish machine's data plate.

The lesson

It's halftime and the line is long. You just cut raw chicken on this board. Now you need it for lettuce. A quick wipe with a towel? Stop. That towel just spreads the germs around. Let's do it the right way.

Cleaning and sanitizing are two different jobs. Cleaning removes the food and dirt you can see. Sanitizing reduces the germs you can't see to safe levels. Always clean first. Sanitizer does not work well on a dirty surface.

When? Clean and sanitize surfaces that touch food when you switch from one raw meat to another, like raw beef to raw chicken. When you switch from raw meat to ready-to-eat food. When you get interrupted and germs may have gotten on it. And if it's used all day with foods that need temperature control, at least every four hours.

No dish machine? Use the three-compartment sink. Start with a clean sink. Step one: scrape food into the trash. Step two: wash in soapy water at 110 degrees Fahrenheit or hotter. Change the water when it gets dirty or cools down.

Step three: rinse in clean water to take off the soap. Step four: sanitize. Leave the items in the sanitizer for the time on the label. Step five: air dry on a clean rack. Never dry with a towel. A towel can put germs right back.

The sanitizer must be the right strength. Chlorine: usually 50 to 100 parts per million. Follow the product label, and use water warm enough for that strength. Quat, short for quaternary ammonium: follow the product label. Iodine: 12.5 to 25 parts per million. Too weak won't kill germs. Too strong can leave chemicals on food. Check it with a test strip every time you mix it, and during your shift.

Wiping cloths for counters stay in the sanitizer bucket between uses. Not on your shoulder. Not on the counter. Keep the bucket off the floor, and change the solution when it gets dirty or the strip reads low.

Using a dish machine? Follow the data plate on the machine. It shows the right temperatures and settings. If the readings don't match, tell your manager before you use it. Your local rules or your venue may be stricter.

The one thing to remember: clean first, then sanitize, and check the strength with a test strip. Scrape, wash, rinse, sanitize, air dry. Clean board, safe food.

Remember

  • Cleaning removes the food and dirt you can see. Sanitizing reduces the germs you can't see to safe levels. Always clean first, then sanitize (FDA Food Code 1-201.10, 4-702.11).
  • Clean and sanitize food-contact surfaces when you switch between different raw animal foods (for example raw beef to raw chicken), when you switch from raw to ready-to-eat food, between raw fruits or vegetables and foods that need temperature control, any time contamination may have happened (for example after an interruption), and at least every 4 hours when they are in constant use with foods that need temperature control (4-602.11).
  • 3-compartment sink: scrape, wash at 110°F or hotter or the temperature on the detergent label (4-501.19), rinse off the soap (4-603.16), sanitize for the contact time on the label (4-703.11), air dry. Never towel dry (4-901.11).
  • Keep the wash, rinse and sanitizer water clean: change it when it gets dirty (4-501.18). Change the wash water when it drops below 110°F (4-501.19).
  • Sanitizer strength (4-501.114): chlorine: follow the label, usually 50–100 ppm; the water must be at least the temperature the Food Code table sets for that strength and pH (4-501.114(A)); quat (quaternary ammonium) as the manufacturer's label says; iodine 12.5–25 ppm. Always check with a test strip (4-501.116).
  • Keep counter wiping cloths in sanitizer between uses, keep the bucket off the floor, and change the solution when it is dirty (3-304.14).
  • Dish machines: run them the way the data plate and the maker's instructions say (4-501.15). Your local rules or your venue may be stricter.

Check yourself

Pick an answer — you will see right away if it is right, and why. Get 4 of 5 right to complete the lesson.

1. A cutting board is used all day with foods that need temperature control. At least how often must it be cleaned and sanitized?

The FDA Food Code (4-602.11(C)) says surfaces in constant use with these foods are cleaned at least every 4 hours, and also when you switch foods or when they may have been contaminated.

2. What is the right order at the 3-compartment sink?

You clean first (scrape, wash, rinse off the soap), then sanitize, then let it air dry, because a towel can put germs back.

3. Unless the detergent label says otherwise, the wash water in the first compartment must be at least…

The FDA Food Code (4-501.19) says the wash water in a sink for washing dishes by hand must be 110°F or hotter, or the temperature on the detergent label, so change it when it cools down.

4. Your chlorine sanitizer should test between…

Chlorine is usually used at 50–100 ppm, following the product label and with water warm enough for that strength (4-501.114), and you always check it with a test strip because the smell tells you nothing.

5. Where should a wet wiping cloth for counters be kept between uses?

The FDA Food Code (3-304.14) says wiping cloths in use are held in sanitizer between uses, and the bucket is kept off the floor.

Answers
  1. 1. At least every 4 hours — The FDA Food Code (4-602.11(C)) says surfaces in constant use with these foods are cleaned at least every 4 hours, and also when you switch foods or when they may have been contaminated.
  2. 2. Scrape, wash, rinse, sanitize, air dry — You clean first (scrape, wash, rinse off the soap), then sanitize, then let it air dry, because a towel can put germs back.
  3. 3. 110°F — The FDA Food Code (4-501.19) says the wash water in a sink for washing dishes by hand must be 110°F or hotter, or the temperature on the detergent label, so change it when it cools down.
  4. 4. 50–100 ppm — Chlorine is usually used at 50–100 ppm, following the product label and with water warm enough for that strength (4-501.114), and you always check it with a test strip because the smell tells you nothing.
  5. 5. In a sanitizer bucket, off the floor — The FDA Food Code (3-304.14) says wiping cloths in use are held in sanitizer between uses, and the bucket is kept off the floor.
Where this comes from
  • Sanitization means applying heat or chemicals to a CLEANED food-contact surface so that disease-causing germs are reduced to safe levels.FDA Food Code 2022, 1-201.10(B), definition of "Sanitization"
  • Utensils and food-contact surfaces of equipment must be sanitized before use after cleaning.FDA Food Code 2022, 4-702.11
  • Food-contact surfaces and utensils must be cleaned before working with a different type of raw animal food, when changing from raw to ready-to-eat food, between raw fruits/vegetables and TCS food, before using or storing a thermometer, and any time during the operation when contamination may have occurred.FDA Food Code 2022, 4-602.11(A)
  • If used with time/temperature control for safety (TCS) food, food-contact surfaces and utensils must be cleaned throughout the day at least every 4 hours (with listed exceptions, such as refrigerated rooms).FDA Food Code 2022, 4-602.11(C) and (D)
  • Manual washing, rinsing and sanitizing requires a sink with at least 3 compartments.FDA Food Code 2022, 4-301.12(A)
  • Sink compartments used for washing and rinsing must be cleaned before use and throughout the day as needed.FDA Food Code 2022, 4-501.14
  • Food debris must be scraped over a waste disposal unit or garbage container (or removed by a machine prewash) before washing.FDA Food Code 2022, 4-603.12
  • The wash solution in manual warewashing equipment must be at least 110°F (43°C) or the temperature on the cleaning agent manufacturer's label.FDA Food Code 2022, 4-501.19
  • Wash, rinse and sanitize solutions must be kept clean.FDA Food Code 2022, 4-501.18
  • Washed items must be rinsed so cleaning chemicals are removed or diluted.FDA Food Code 2022, 4-603.16
  • Chemical sanitizing contact times must follow the EPA-registered label instructions.FDA Food Code 2022, 4-703.11(C)
  • Chemical sanitizers must be used according to the EPA-registered label use instructions. Chlorine: the required minimum water temperature depends on the concentration and pH (25–49 ppm needs 120°F; 50–99 ppm needs 100°F at pH 10 or less, or 75°F at pH 8 or less; 100 ppm needs 55°F); iodine: 12.5–25 ppm, at least 68°F, pH 5.0 or less unless the label says otherwise; quaternary ammonium: concentration per the manufacturer's label, at least 75°F, water hardness 500 ppm or less or as the label says.FDA Food Code 2022, 4-501.114 and 7-204.11
  • Sanitizer concentration must be measured accurately with a test kit or other device, and such a test kit must be provided.FDA Food Code 2022, 4-501.116 and 4-302.14
  • In-use wiping cloths for counters and equipment surfaces must be held between uses in sanitizer solution at the 4-501.114 concentration and laundered daily; the cloths and solution must be free of food debris and visible soil; the containers must be stored off the floor.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-304.14(B), (D), (E)
  • After cleaning and sanitizing, equipment and utensils must be air-dried (or used after adequate draining) and may not be cloth-dried.FDA Food Code 2022, 4-901.11
  • A warewashing (dish) machine must be operated according to its data plate and the manufacturer's instructions; the data plate lists the design and operating specifications such as temperatures and pressures.FDA Food Code 2022, 4-501.15 and 4-204.113
