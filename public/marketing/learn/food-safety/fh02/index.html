Personal hygiene and handwashing

about 3 min read5-question check

In this lesson

  • Know when to wash your hands at work.
  • Wash your hands the right way, for at least 20 seconds.
  • Keep the hand sink clear, stocked, and used only for hands.
  • Use utensils or gloves, never bare hands, with ready-to-eat food, and change gloves at the right times.
  • Follow the rules for hair, jewelry, clothes, eating, and drinking.
  • Know which symptoms and illnesses (the Big 6) to report to your supervisor.

The lesson

Halftime rush. A coworker comes out of the restroom, skips the sink, and grabs a bun with bare hands. Germs you can't see make guests sick. I'm Inspector Dex. Let's fix this.

When do you wash? Before you start food prep. After the restroom. After raw meat, before ready-to-eat food. After trash, dirty dishes, or touching your face, hair or phone. After you cough, sneeze, eat, drink or smoke. When you change tasks. And before putting on gloves.

Wet hands with warm, running water. Add soap. Scrub at least 10 to 15 seconds: backs of hands, between fingers, under the nails. Rinse. Dry with a single-use paper towel, and turn off the faucet with it. The whole wash takes at least 20 seconds. Hand sanitizer never replaces washing.

The hand sink is only for hands. No ice, dishes or mop water. Nothing blocking it. Keep soap and paper towels stocked; if they run out, tell your supervisor. I check it at every inspection.

Ready-to-eat food gets no more cooking or washing before it's eaten: buns, fries, salad, cut fruit, a finished hot dog. Never touch it with bare hands. Use tongs, deli paper, a scoop, or single-use gloves.

Gloves are single-use. Wash before you put them on. Change them when they tear, get dirty, after any interruption like taking cash, or when you switch tasks, like raw burgers to buns: gloves off, wash, new pair. Never wash gloves to reuse them.

Hair goes under a hat or hairnet; beards under a beard net. No watches, bracelets or rings, except a plain band. Your local rules or your venue may be stricter. Wear clean clothes and a clean apron.

Eat, drink and smoke only in the break area. A closed drink with a lid may be allowed, away from food.

Feeling sick? Tell your supervisor before your shift, or right away if it starts at work. Report vomiting, diarrhea, yellow skin or eyes, a sore throat with fever, or a cut or boil with pus. With vomiting or diarrhea, you must stay home. For anything else, your supervisor follows the rules: home, or other work.

Also tell them if a doctor says you have one of the Big 6: norovirus, hepatitis A, Shigella, Shiga toxin-producing E. coli, Salmonella Typhi, or nontyphoidal Salmonella.

A small cut on your hand? Cover it with a waterproof bandage or finger cot, then a glove over it.

The one thing to remember: clean hands protect every guest. Wash right, no bare hands on ready-to-eat food, and speak up when you're sick.

Remember

  • Wash your hands right before you start working with food, after the restroom, after raw meat, trash, dirty dishes, or touching your face, hair or phone, after coughing, sneezing, eating, drinking or smoking, when you change tasks, and before you put on gloves (2-301.14).
  • A handwash = warm running water, soap, scrub at least 10–15 seconds (backs, between fingers, under nails), rinse, dry with a single-use paper towel. At least 20 seconds in all (2-301.12).
  • Hand sanitizer never replaces handwashing (2-301.16).
  • The hand sink is only for washing hands, never blocked, and always stocked with soap and paper towels, or another hand dryer your stand has (5-205.11, 6-301.11, 6-301.12).
  • Never touch ready-to-eat food with bare hands. Use tongs, deli paper, a scoop, or single-use gloves (3-301.11).
  • Gloves are single-use: wash before putting them on; change them when they tear, get dirty, after an interruption (like handling money), or when you switch tasks (3-304.15).
  • Hair under a hat or hairnet, beards under a beard net; no jewelry on hands or arms except a plain band; clean clothes and apron (2-402.11, 2-303.11, 2-304.11).
  • Eat, drink and smoke only in the break area; a closed drink with a lid may be allowed (2-401.11).
  • Tell your supervisor before your shift, or right away if it starts at work, about vomiting, diarrhea, yellow skin or eyes, sore throat with fever, an infected cut or boil, or a Big 6 diagnosis (2-201.11). Also tell them if someone you live with has one of the Big 6. With vomiting or diarrhea, you must stay home (2-201.12).
  • Cover a cut on your hand with a waterproof bandage or finger cot and a glove over it; if the cut is infected with pus, also tell your supervisor (2-201.11). Your local rules or your venue may be stricter.
  • Hand sinks need water at least 100°F (38°C) (5-202.12). The SDX app flags a hand sink below 95°F — aim for 100°F or more.

Check yourself

Pick an answer — you will see right away if it is right, and why. Get 4 of 5 right to complete the lesson.

1. How long should a full handwash take?

The FDA Food Code (2-301.12) says at least 20 seconds in all, with at least 10 to 15 seconds of scrubbing with soap.

2. What is the hand sink used for?

A hand sink is only for handwashing (5-205.11), and it must stay clear and stocked with soap and paper towels.

3. A guest orders fries. How do you put them in the basket?

Fries are ready-to-eat, so no bare-hand contact (3-301.11): use a utensil, deli paper, or clean single-use gloves.

4. You worked with raw burger patties. Now you need to handle buns. What do you do with your gloves?

Gloves are single-use (3-304.15), and when you switch from raw food to ready-to-eat food you wash your hands before new gloves (2-301.14).

5. Your doctor says you have norovirus. What should you do?

Norovirus is one of the Big 6: you must report it to the person in charge (2-201.11), who must then exclude or restrict you under the Food Code rules (2-201.12).

  2. 2. Only washing hands — A hand sink is only for handwashing (5-205.11), and it must stay clear and stocked with soap and paper towels.
  3. 3. With a scoop, tongs, or a gloved hand — Fries are ready-to-eat, so no bare-hand contact (3-301.11): use a utensil, deli paper, or clean single-use gloves.

Where this comes from
  • Food employees must clean their hands and exposed arms immediately before engaging in food preparation (including working with exposed food, clean equipment and utensils, and unwrapped single-service and single-use articles), and also after touching bare body parts, after using the toilet room, after coughing, sneezing, using a tissue, using tobacco, eating or drinking, after handling soiled equipment or utensils, during food preparation as often as needed and when changing tasks, when switching between raw and ready-to-eat food, before putting on gloves to work with food, and after any other activity that contaminates the hands.FDA Food Code 2022, 2-301.14 When to Wash
  • Hands and exposed arms must be cleaned for at least 20 seconds: rinse under clean, running warm water, apply soap, rub vigorously for at least 10 to 15 seconds (including under fingernails, fingertips and between fingers), rinse thoroughly, then dry thoroughly.FDA Food Code 2022, 2-301.12(A)-(B) Cleaning Procedure
  • To avoid recontaminating hands, employees may use disposable paper towels or a similar clean barrier to touch surfaces such as manually operated faucet handles.FDA Food Code 2022, 2-301.12(C)
  • Hands may be dried with individual disposable towels, a continuous towel system, or an approved hand-drying device such as a heated-air or air-knife dryer.FDA Food Code 2022, 6-301.12 Hand Drying Provision
  • Hand antiseptics (hand sanitizer) may be used only after hands have been cleaned as required; they do not replace handwashing.FDA Food Code 2022, 2-301.16 Hand Antiseptics
  • A handwashing sink must be accessible at all times for employee use and may not be used for purposes other than handwashing.FDA Food Code 2022, 5-205.11 Using a Handwashing Sink
  • Each handwashing sink must be provided with a supply of hand cleaning soap and a hand-drying provision.FDA Food Code 2022, 6-301.11 Handwashing Cleanser, Availability; 6-301.12 Hand Drying Provision
  • A sign or poster that tells food employees to wash their hands must be provided at all handwashing sinks used by food employees.FDA Food Code 2022, 6-301.14 Handwashing Signage
  • If disposable towels are used at handwashing sinks, a waste receptacle must be located at each sink or group of adjacent sinks.FDA Food Code 2022, 5-501.16(B) Storage Areas, Rooms, and Receptacles, Capacity and Availability
  • Ready-to-eat food is food that is edible without additional preparation to achieve food safety (no more washing or cooking is needed to make it safe).FDA Food Code 2022, 1-201.10(B) Definitions — 'Ready-to-eat food'
  • Food employees may not contact exposed, ready-to-eat food with their bare hands and shall use suitable utensils such as deli tissue, spatulas, tongs, single-use gloves, or dispensing equipment.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-301.11(B) Preventing Contamination from Hands
  • Single-use gloves are used for only one task, used for no other purpose, and discarded when damaged or soiled, or when interruptions occur in the operation.FDA Food Code 2022, 3-304.15(A) Gloves, Use Limitation
  • Food employees must wear hair restraints such as hats, hair coverings or nets, and beard restraints that keep hair from contacting exposed food, clean equipment, utensils, linens, and unwrapped single-service articles.FDA Food Code 2022, 2-402.11 Hair Restraints, Effectiveness
  • Except for a plain ring such as a wedding band, food employees may not wear jewelry, including medical information jewelry, on their arms and hands while preparing food.FDA Food Code 2022, 2-303.11 Prohibition (Jewelry)
  • Food employees must wear clean outer clothing to prevent contamination of food, equipment, utensils, linens, and single-service and single-use articles.FDA Food Code 2022, 2-304.11 Clean Condition (Outer Clothing)
  • Employees may eat, drink, and use any form of tobacco only in designated areas where contamination cannot result; a food employee may drink from a closed beverage container if it is handled to prevent contamination.FDA Food Code 2022, 2-401.11(A)-(B) Eating, Drinking, or Using Tobacco
  • Food employees must report to the person in charge the symptoms of vomiting, diarrhea, jaundice, sore throat with fever, or a lesion containing pus such as a boil or infected wound that is open or draining.FDA Food Code 2022, 2-201.11(A)(1) Responsibility of Permit Holder, Person in Charge, and Conditional Employees
  • Food employees must report a diagnosis of norovirus, hepatitis A virus, Shigella spp., Shiga toxin-producing Escherichia coli, Salmonella Typhi (typhoid fever), or nontyphoidal Salmonella (the 'Big 6').FDA Food Code 2022, 2-201.11(A)(2)
  • Food employees must also report certain exposures to the Big 6, such as being linked to a confirmed disease outbreak or living in the same household as a person diagnosed with a Big 6 illness.FDA Food Code 2022, 2-201.11(A)(4)-(5)
  • A lesion containing pus (a boil or infected wound that is open or draining) on the hands or wrists must be reported unless it is protected by an impermeable cover such as a finger cot or stall with a single-use glove worn over it.FDA Food Code 2022, 2-201.11(A)(1)(e)(i)
  • The person in charge must exclude or restrict a food employee as 2-201.12 requires; an employee who is symptomatic with vomiting or diarrhea must be excluded, except when the symptom is from a noninfectious condition.FDA Food Code 2022, 2-201.12(A) Exclusions and Restrictions
  • An employee excluded for vomiting or diarrhea who has not been diagnosed with one of the Big 6 may be reinstated after being free of symptoms for at least 24 hours, or with written medical documentation that the symptom is from a noninfectious condition. A diagnosed infection (norovirus, hepatitis A, Shigella, Shiga toxin-producing E. coli, Salmonella Typhi or nontyphoidal Salmonella) follows the later paragraphs of 2-201.13, each with its own conditions for coming back.FDA Food Code 2022, 2-201.13(A)(1) (symptoms, no diagnosis); diagnosed infections 2-201.13(B)–(G)
  • Norovirus is the leading cause of illness and outbreaks from contaminated food in the United States.CDC, Norovirus (cdc.gov/norovirus) — Norovirus Facts and Stats
  • A handwashing sink must provide water at a temperature of at least 100°F (38°C), through a mixing valve or combination faucet.FDA Food Code 2022, 5-202.12(A)
